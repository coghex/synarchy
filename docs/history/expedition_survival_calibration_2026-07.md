# Expedition Survival Calibration

## Purpose

`docs/expedition_gameplay_loop.md` step 7 ("Tune survival around the journey")
asks the first expedition three questions:

- is there enough water?
- is there enough food?
- can the party survive one injury?

This document records what the game actually does when those questions are put
to it, using the manual scenarios from #925. It is an **observation log first
and a tuning record second**: every gameplay outcome below is evidence for
review, never a pass/fail threshold. The scenario runner deliberately stays
outside `tools/run_probes.py`, `tools/ci_probes.py`, and CI, and its exit
status reports setup/runtime failure only — never a balance verdict.

Balance values are only changed here after the observations below have been
presented to the project owner and explicitly approved (#919 requirement 6),
and only when the value is already a named tunable (#919 requirement 3).

## Scenario 1 — expedition (water and food)

### Setup as run

- World: seed 42, size 64, plates 3 (deterministic, generated by the run).
- Base camp derived deterministically at `(15, -16)`; surface z 27.
- Roster: the real starting party — 5 acolytes + 1 technomule, player faction,
  uids 1–6, spawned with each def's real `starting_inventory`.
- Route: 3 outbound legs of 8 tiles east (24 tiles out), then straight back —
  48 tiles total.
- The technomule (uid 6) stayed at camp as the supply point for the whole run.
  It never travelled.
- Scenario condition: each acolyte's standing `find_water` goal was retired at
  spawn, so the fixed route is the standing order. This is the same condition
  every probe in `tools/` applies, and it means the run does **not** exercise
  autonomous water-seeking.

### Provisioning choices at the stationary technomule

Transferred off the mule through the real engine path
(`unit.transferItemToUnit`, the same verb the fetch AI uses), stopping at each
receiver's carrying capacity:

- acolyte 1: 3x `rations`, 1x `first_aid_kit`
- acolyte 2: 3x `rations`

No water was transferred. The mule's 20x `water` are recipe-input material,
not a drinkable supply; each acolyte's hydration reserve is the single filled
2 L `canteen_steel_2l` it spawns with. The mule kept 14x `rations` in reserve.

### The two travellers were not comparable units

The two acolytes the scenario picked differ by roughly 3.6x in body scale,
which shapes every observation below:

| | acolyte 1 (Isolde Vellan) | acolyte 2 (Valen Farrow) |
|---|---|---|
| body mass (from `max_hydration` / 0.6) | ~38 kg | ~138 kg |
| `max_hydration` | 22.8 L | 82.8 L |
| `max_calories` | 758.8 kcal | 2760.0 kcal |
| blood volume | 2.85 L | 10.35 L |
| `carrying_capacity` | 9.3 kg | 31.9 kg |
| load after provisioning | 11.3 kg (**121 %**) | 12.4 kg (39 %) |
| observed travel speed | 0.19–0.22 tiles/s | 0.29 tiles/s |

This is the ordinary spread of `data/units/acolyte.yaml`'s body block
(`height` 1.3–2.3 m, `bulk` 0.5–1.5, `bodyfat` 0.02–0.38), not a scenario
artifact.

Elapsed times are real seconds from the start of the run.

| checkpoint | acolyte 1 hydration | acolyte 2 hydration | acolyte 1 stomach | acolyte 2 stomach |

- **Inventory.** Acolyte 1 ended with `rations`x3 (from 5); acolyte 2 ended
  with **no rations at all** (from 5). Both canteens were still at full
  `fill 2.00` at every checkpoint, including the last. The first-aid kit stayed
  on acolyte 1 for the whole trip. The mule's inventory was unchanged from the
  moment of provisioning.
- **Hydration.** Acolyte 1 lost 3.1 L of 22.8 (13.6 % of its pool) across the
  whole 460 s round trip; acolyte 2 lost 12.3 L of 82.8 (14.9 %). Neither ever
  drank: both finished at ~85 % hydration, just inside `drink_min_thirst`
  (0.2 thirst), so `drink_from_canteen` never fired.
- **Hunger / calories.** The calorie STORE never moved — both units held
  100 % of `max_calories` at every single checkpoint (758.8→759.0 and

- **Exhaustion.** Acolyte 1: 8.4 → 7.4. Acolyte 2: 10.5 → 3.9. Neither

- **Stamina.** Acolyte 1 ran at 1.9–3.5 of 8.5 for the entire journey — a
  direct consequence of walking at 121 % of capacity. Acolyte 2 stayed pinned

- **Injuries.** None, on either unit, at any checkpoint.
- **Treatment activity.** None — no wound, no medic claim, `treating -`

- **Return state.** Acolyte 1 returned to camp: `(15.42, -15.58)`, 0.6 tiles

  from camp** (see observation E3).

### What the water and food questions actually answered

- **Is there enough water?** On this route, overwhelmingly yes, and the
  question never became live: an 85 %-full unit never reaches the drink
  threshold, so the single spawn canteen was never touched. At the shipped
  drain (`drain_constant_frac = 0.01/42` per second, activity-scaled) an
  average pool empties in ~3 game-days; a 460 s round trip spends ~15 % of it.
- **Is there enough food?** Also yes for survival — the calorie store never
  dropped below 100 % — but the carried *reserve* did not survive the trip.
  Both acolytes crossed the `eat_max_fraction` (0.25) stomach threshold on the
  return leg and each ate a full meal in one event, because `eatExecute`
  (`scripts/unit_ai_needs.lua`) keeps feeding until the stomach is ≥ 99 % full.
  With 250 kcal discrete rations, acolyte 2's 1380 kcal stomach consumed **all
  five rations in a single sitting** (the last one clamped at max, wasting
  ~215 kcal), leaving it with zero food 25 tiles from camp. Acolyte 1's
  379 kcal stomach took two, wasting ~216 kcal of the second.

## Scenario 2 — first-aid (surviving one injury)

### Setup as run

- World: `movement_arena` page `move_test`, with a 2-z loam ridge at
  x −4..0, y −20..20. 2 z is the **shallowest drop the mover turns into a
  real fall at all** (`pcFallTriggerDrop` is 2).
- Roster: the same real starting party, uids 1–6.
- Scout: acolyte 1 (~104.7 kg body, 7.85 L blood). Medic: acolyte 2.
- Kit issue: 1x `first_aid_kit` moved mule → scout via
  `unit.transferItemToUnit`, so the exact stocked kit that spawns on the
  technomule is the one in play.

### First-aid-kit state

| | contents |
|---|---|
| before | held by unit 1: `tweezers`x1, `gauze`x10, `elastic_wrap`x5, `antibiotics`x1, `bandage`x10, `scissors`x1, `antiseptic`x1 |
| after | held by unit 1: `tweezers`x1, `gauze`x10, `elastic_wrap`x5, `antibiotics`x1, **`bandage`x9**, `scissors`x1, `antiseptic`x1 |

One bandage was consumed — a real supply mutation, from the real spawned kit.

### The injury

The 2-z fall produced **33 wounds** in one landing:

- 21 blunt wounds, every one at severity **0.40** — that is `bruiseCap`, i.e.
  all of them are pinned at the model's maximum bruise severity;
- 12 fractures: skull 0.16, windpipe 0.26, sternum 0.20, l_thumb 0.44,
  r_palm 0.27, l_thigh 0.17, r_thigh 0.17, l_sole 0.46, r_sole 0.46, plus
  their paired entries;
- across skull, windpipe, sternum, both biceps, both hands, both thighs and
  both soles;
- aggregate bleed **0.865 L/s** against a 7.85 L blood volume — an
  exsanguination time of roughly **9 seconds**;
- pain 16.99, pose `collapsed`, knocked down.

For reference, `src/Unit/Fall.hs`'s own tunable block documents its intended
calibration as: a baseline acolyte "walks off ≤1-z; is bruised + knocked down
at 2–4-z; takes its first ankle/leg fracture around 5–6-z". The observed 2-z
landing produced twelve fractures, including a skull, a windpipe and a
sternum. The observation is recorded here as a discrepancy between the
module's documented target and its live behavior; it is not scored as a
failure.

### The treatment

`unit.treatBleeding(medic 2, patient 1, kit owner 1)` was administered
immediately on the injury being observed, through the same engine verb the
medic AI itself calls:

```
ok=True, part=skull, kind=blunt, method=bandage,
bandagesUsed=1, attempts=1, seep=0.053, message='treated'
```

- The treatment **succeeded** and dressed exactly **one** of the 33 wounds
  (skull blunt), cutting that wound's seep to 0.053.
- The medic AI on acolyte 2 independently **claimed the scout as a patient**
  (`action treat_ally`, `treating 1`) and held that claim for the rest of the
  run — the arbitration path works.
- Aggregate bleed barely moved: 0.865 → 0.818 → 0.811 L/s. Blood fell
  7.15 → 5.70 → 4.31 L (55 % remaining) across the ~4 s of observation, still
  bleeding at 0.81 L/s when the run ended.
- Post-treatment interval: the scout stood back up (`pose standing`,
  `anim injured_walk`, `action follow_command`) carrying 33 undressed-but-one
  wounds at 55 % blood.

### What the injury question actually answered

One dressing per `treatBleeding` call against 33 simultaneous wounds, at an
aggregate 0.865 L/s on a 7.85 L reserve, is a treatment throughput far below
the bleed rate: the patient's whole blood volume is gone in ~9 s, while each
treatment action retires one wound. The dominant term is not fracture
severity but the **count** of capped 0.40 bruises: at
`kindBleedFactor "blunt" = 0.2` and `bleedScale = 1.2`, ~21 of them sum to
most of the 0.865 L/s.

## Observations (not verdicts)

Recorded per #919 requirement 2, as observations rather than failed
thresholds.

- **E1 — a small acolyte cannot carry its own kit.** Acolyte 1 (9.3 kg
  capacity) shed `pick_steel` and `shovel_steel` at spawn under the documented
  spawn-time capacity check, and provisioning it off the mule still left it at
  **121 % of capacity** for the whole journey. The encumbrance penalty
  (`ENC_OVER_K`) then roughly halved its speed and held its stamina at
  ~2 / 8.5 for 460 s.
- **E2 — the technomule spawns over its own base capacity.** Engine log:
  `UnitSpawn UnitId 6: loadout 171.3721 kg exceeds capacity 163.28827 kg with
  nothing left to shed` (its `starting_inventory` declares no `drop_priority`,
  so nothing is sheddable). The live modified capacity reads 244.9 kg, so the
  spawn-time shed check compared against the unmodified base rather than the
  mule's `+50 %` cybernetic modifier. No gameplay consequence was observed in
  either scenario.
- **E3 — a completed move order does not hold position.** Acolyte 2 was in
  `action wander` at every single checkpoint, never `follow_command`, moving at
  exactly its meander speed (0.29 tiles/s). It overshot outbound leg 3 by
  4.7 tiles, and after the return leg it drifted **10.7 tiles back out of
  camp**. It did pass through each waypoint — the runner recorded arrival — but
  a player watching this would not describe the party as having returned
  together.
- **E4 — a meal empties the pack.** Both travellers ate to a ~99 % full
  stomach in a single event on the return leg, spending 2 of 5 and **5 of 5**
  rations respectively, with ~215 kcal of the final ration in each meal wasted
  to the stomach-capacity clamp. Acolyte 2 finished the expedition with no food
  at all.
- **E5 — the first-aid scout fell before it was told to.** The checkpoint
  labelled "kit issued, before the fall" already shows the scout `collapsed`
  with all 33 wounds and an active bleed, at `(-4.50, 1.50)` — off the **west**
  edge of the ridge, opposite the commanded eastward descent. It walked off
  under `action wander` during setup. The scenario's intended pre-fall baseline
  was therefore never captured, and the reported landing position is not the
  commanded one. This is a staging observation about the scenario (#925's
  contract), recorded here rather than fixed.
- **E6 — a critically bled unit stands up and walks.** With 33 wounds, 55 %
  blood and an 0.81 L/s bleed, the scout returned to `pose standing` and
  resumed following its move order.
- **E7 — the canteen drain path is name-based.** `scripts/unit_ai_needs.lua`
  drains canteen fill with `unit.modifyItemFill` (by def name) although
  `unit.modifyItemFillById` exists for exact-instance mutation. This did not
  surface in either run — each acolyte carries exactly one canteen and neither
  ever drank — so it is recorded as a latent observation only, per #919
  requirement 4's prohibition on changing inventory-transfer semantics here.

## Fall-trauma calibration (#998)

Scenario 2 above measured ONE specific spawned scout (~104.7 kg, presumably
constitution/toughness near 1.0) taking a 2-z fall and recorded 33 wounds,
0.865 L/s aggregate initial bleed, and a projected ~9 s exsanguination — far
beyond `src/Unit/Fall.hs`'s own documented target ("bruised + knocked down at
2–4-z; first ankle/leg fracture around 5–6-z"). #998 repairs the fall-trauma
model only; autonomous ledge avoidance is follow-up 5 above, not this issue.

### Method

Random spawn stats make "a starting acolyte" ambiguous, so #998 fixes THREE
deterministic profiles — the exact clamp endpoints of
`data/units/acolyte.yaml`'s `rollStat` ranges for height/bulk/toughness — and
tests each independently rather than assuming the frail profile is the
"vulnerable" one:

| profile | height | bulk | toughness | body mass (22·h²·b) | blood volume (mass×0.075) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| frail   | 1.3 | 0.5 | 0.8 | 18.59 kg | 1.39 L |
| average | 1.8 | 1.0 | 1.0 | 71.28 kg | 5.35 L |
| extreme | 2.3 | 1.5 | 1.2 | 174.57 kg | 13.09 L |

Constitution is pinned at 1.0 for all three (it also scales aggregate bleed
via `Combat.Wounds.Bleed`'s clamped-constitution divisor, and acolyte
constitution otherwise rolls 0.5–1.5). Every fall wound is measured with the
same initial state the engine actually stamps
(`src/Unit/Thread/Movement.hs`): `woundBandage = 1.0`, `woundClot = 0.0`,
`woundHeal = 0.0` — untreated, unclotted. "Aggregate initial bleed" is
`Combat.Wounds.Bleed.bleedRateFor` over that wound set; "exsanguination"
is blood volume ÷ that rate, the same naive (no clotting, no treatment)
projection the original ~9 s figure used. Body parts come from the shipped
YAML through `Engine.Asset.YamlUnits.unitYamlBodyPartToBodyPart` — the same
conversion the Lua unit loader uses — never a hand-transcribed fixture; see
`test-headless/Test/Headless/Unit/Fall.hs`.

**Vitality is heart-only and it is unreachable at 2-z.** The shipped acolyte
flags only `heart` (torso, depth 0.50) as `vital: true`; a 2-z fall's reach is
`0.30 + 0.05×2 = 0.40`, so the heart cannot be hit regardless of tuning.
Worse: the torso's deterministic (RNG-free) weighted organ pick was measured
to land on `lungs`/`intestines` at every tested drop height from 2-z through
40-z — the heart is never the selected organ AT ANY HEIGHT on this body
topology, a fact about `Unit.Injury.allocateSubparts`'s fixed hash-based
picks and the acolyte's per-organ `area_weight`s, not about severity tuning.
A fall therefore cannot trigger the vital-injury death rule on the shipped
acolyte at all. "No vital injury" is consequently NOT a meaningful pass/fail
signal for this contract; the operative measurements below are wound
count/kind, aggregate bleed relative to blood volume, and knockdown.

### What changed in `src/Unit/Fall.hs`

- `energyToSeverity`: `0.040` → `0.006`, retuned against the shipped topology
  so the documented average acolyte's fracture ladder holds (below).
- `ensureBone`: previously injected a synthetic 18 mm bone layer into ANY
  part lacking a literal `"bone"` material — including the neck's cartilage
  windpipe and the torso's boneless organs (liver, stomach, intestines) and
  boneless carotid artery, none of which are extremities and none of which
  should be able to "fracture". Every current extremity (hands, feet, limbs)
  already carries a real bone layer in `data/units/acolyte.yaml`, so the
  synthetic layer was firing ONLY on anatomically boneless soft tissue,
  double-counting those parts' wounds. Broadened to skip when the part has
  ANY structural/organic tissue (bone, cartilage, organ, nerve, artery), not
  just bone.
- `partInjuries`: previously emitted ONE wound per tissue LAYER, so a single
  strike to a limb (skin + fat + muscle, all "flesh" → all "blunt") produced
  THREE separate capped bruises instead of one. Now groups by injury KIND
  first (summing the grouped layers' failure-energy, i.e. their combined
  resistance, before dividing the load) — mirroring how
  `Combat.Resolution.Damage`'s `distOf` already groups a weapon strike's
  per-layer deposits by kind. This was the dominant driver of the
  33-wound/0.865 L/s pathology: not fracture severity, but redundant
  same-kind wound multiplicity from one impact to one part.

`kindBleedFactor` and `bleedScale` (`src/Combat/Wounds/*.hs`) were NOT
touched — shared combat constants, #916's territory per #998's own scope.
No `data/units/*.yaml` body data was touched.

### Fracture ladder (independently measured per profile)

The average profile now matches `Unit.Fall`'s own documented target (first
fracture around 5–6-z); the extreme and frail profiles are independently
measured and diverge from it in the physically expected direction — mass
drives impact energy (`E = mass·g·h`), so the heavier/tougher extreme profile
fractures earlier and the much-lighter frail profile fractures far later,
despite its lower toughness stat:

| profile | first fracture | first concussion |

| frail (18.59 kg) | 15-z | not observed through 40-z |
| average (71.28 kg) | 5-z | 22-z |
| extreme (174.57 kg) | 3-z | 12-z |

The frail profile is the **permitted exception** #998 allows ("may die at
2-z if physiologically justified, deterministic, and explicitly documented")
— but on this shipped topology it measures as the SAFEST profile at 2-z, not
the most vulnerable: its far lower body mass reduces impact energy more than
its lower toughness costs it. No profile actually exercises the death
exception at 2-z; this corrects the naive "frail = vulnerable" framing.
