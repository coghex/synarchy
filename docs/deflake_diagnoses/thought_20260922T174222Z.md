## Causal explanation and evidence limits

`tools/thought_probe.py:231` observes the recently healthy unit's mood near
1.0 immediately after injuring it. `brain.lua` intentionally drifts mood
slowly (`MOOD_DRIFT = 0.02`); pain does not instantly make it miserable.
`scripts/thoughts.lua` then gives negative entries the minimum mood bias
0.25. The two pain thoughts' base weights 3 and 2 become only 0.75 and 0.5,
while the positive random competitor nearly doubles. The default 20-attempt
budget at `thought_probe.py:201` therefore permits ordinary valid draws to
fail the test. High pain never promises a pain thought within that cap.

This fixture/expectation defect is demonstrated with high confidence.
The identical high starting mood in both original failures and the matching
controlled miss behavior strongly support it as their explanation. Exact
historical roll-by-roll attribution remains unavailable: the original
artifacts contain neither pain/eligibility snapshots nor their sample lists.

That missing evidence has a concrete cause: `report_samples` sends outcomes
through `rep.note`, but `probe_protocol.Reporter.note` deliberately suppresses
them in protocol mode. The failure check detail retains only "20 rolls,
none in the 'state' category". Retained engine logs cannot recover those draws.

The mood assertion is also not causally isolated. It compares separate reads
around the entire multi-roll loop, mixing random-thought deltas and background
`brain.tick` drift with any state thought. Run 7 passes it despite emitting no
state thought, directly demonstrating that it does not prove its named claim.
Run 3's small aggregate delta does not establish a broken thought mood writer;
all 2,971 successful controlled batches observed the correct immediate delta.

No production thought-system defect was found in these experiments. No
introducing commit was established, and the original 2/10 rate should not be
replaced by the controlled 29/1,000 result.

## Corrective direction

Give phase 3 the same deliberate treatment as the already-controlled cold
phase: verify high pain and the competing trigger inputs, hold a stated mood
for each selection, and choose a bounded roll budget justified by the eligible
weights. Preserve the real shipped catalogue and ordinary selection. For the
claimed high-pain behavior, checking the emitted pain thought's identity would
also prevent unrelated state categories from satisfying the assertion.

Measure mood immediately before and after the selected state-producing tick
in the same Lua chunk, checking its actual authored delta; do not grade the
net drift over unrelated rolls. Store eligibility, every sample, and those
paired mood values in structured check details so flake evidence retains them.
Do not weaken the mood threshold, hide failures behind retries, or change
production weights merely to satisfy this probe.

A future repair should retain all other checks, validate the original full
probe repeatedly under matching conditions, and cover a negative case that
fails when a thought's mood effect is absent. These experiments are diagnosis
evidence, not canonical committed repair verification. No issue, PR, or
publication was requested; the census measurement and tolerance stay intact.
